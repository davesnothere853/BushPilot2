01 Site plan

One square where everything the game does can be exercised in thirty seconds of taxiing. The whole square is the new ground: one continuous surface, fine everywhere, dig or push anywhere — no patches, no conversion, ever. Past the fence line the plain just keeps going so you can fly out, turn round, and shoot approaches.

02 Mood frames — the look we're testing

The Dead Stick direction, translated to our island: always weather, never a blue-sky default. Low light, wet surfaces, fog sitting in the low ground. These four frames are the shots the prototype has to be able to produce.

04 Build order

  1. 1The ground itself — one chunked, always-fine, fully deformable field with the zone materials painted in. The cube-pop problem dies here, by design.
  2. 2The weather and light — overcast sky, valley fog, wet-surface response. Shots compared against Frame A before anything else goes in.
  3. 3Machines on it — dozer and excavator wired straight to the new ground; cut the Road Nº1, dig the first pit, bench the knoll.
  4. 4The plane on it — same flight model, circuits off the gravel, off-the-edge flight, land back.
  5. 5Judgment day — side-by-side renders vs the Dead Stick references. You call it: good enough in Godot, or we take the logic and go engine shopping with evidence.

Everything proved here scales to the island later — the square is the new terrain system, just small. Nothing in the current game gets touched until you say so.

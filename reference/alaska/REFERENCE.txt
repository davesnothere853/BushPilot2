ALASKA BUSH STRIPS AND RIVER VALLEYS - references (DESIGN_NOTES 227)
====================================================================
Dave: gather pictures of Alaskan bush strips, the river valleys, the gravel
beds in the rivers. Use them for the landing places and the valleys.

Bush strips (alaskamagazine.com "remote airstrips are never long enough";
mountainflying.com backcountry landings; flyingmag.com "Nowhere to hide"):
- "Two sizes: short, and shorter" - about 600 to 1,100 ft (180-340 m).
- Natural ones: river gravel bars, beaches, tundra ridges. Built ones: cleared
  by hand from brush and forest. Soft spots, swampy ends, snow, soft sand.
- Brush grows back fast; floods wash bar strips away and cut channels through
  them (one was rebuilt 3/4 mile down the river).
- Mountain strips slope (land uphill, take off down), often one-way, trees at
  the ends, hidden until short final, winds shifting near the ground.
- Lodges: a strip or a lake beside the lodge; float planes at a dock.
- Villages: a gravel strip beside the houses, the fuel drums, the windsock.
- Mining camps: a strip bulldozed on the valley floor or a bench by the
  workings.

River valleys (fws.gov Tlikakila River; Copper River braided-river study):
- Textbook U-shaped glacial valleys: steep rock walls, peaks 1,500-1,800 m
  above, hanging glaciers on the peaks, side streams falling over 30 m+
  waterfalls or cutting narrow gorges, sheer cliffs along the sides.
- The upper valley forested and narrow; lower down the river spreads across a
  broad flat floor and braids into many channels.
- Braiding comes from heavy gravel load, high flow, steep grade, banks that
  wash away; width more than 50 x depth. Glacial water is grey-green, silty.
- Gravel bars: long low lens-shaped islands between the channels, bare grey
  gravel nearest the water, willow and alder scrub on the older higher bars,
  spruce on the oldest; cut banks where a channel swings into the terrace;
  river terraces stepping up to the forest. The bigger bars are where small
  planes land.
- Pictures shown to Dave in chat (image search): Alaska bush strips in the
  mountains, Super Cub ridge landings, lodge strips and float planes, gravel
  bar landings (Knik River), village strips, braided rivers with willow
  islands.

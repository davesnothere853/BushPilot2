HOME STRIP - Dave's bush flying base, just outside town (1982)
==============================================================
Dave: a small dirt runway down a dirt road on the outskirts of town; a small
hangar with basic living quarters he lives out of, a cot in the corner. He runs
a one-plane bush flying company (cargo to transport missions) and is the
volunteer forest firefighter. As detailed as possible. Interiors of the era,
TVs, phones, magazines, signs, billboards: make it feel alive.

Real model for the place: Hope Aerodrome (CYHE), 4.8 km west of Hope townsite
in Flood: one turf runway 07/25, 1,207 m x 76 m, elevation 39 m; built c.1942
(RCAF / Dept of Transport); a gliding club; Hope has a Forest Service initial
attack crew base and a helitack base.  [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope_Aerodrome,
blog.gov.bc.ca/bcwildfire/fraser-initial-attack-crew-coastal-fire-centre]
Ours is smaller and private: a dirt strip ~600 m x 18 m.

Era interiors (1970s - early 80s), from the sources gathered:
- Walls: dark faux-wood panelling (rec rooms, dens, offices), plywood in work
  buildings; brass-coloured switch plates; calendars, charts, notices pinned up.
- Floors: shag carpet (harvest gold, avocado, rust, brown) in homes; sheet
  linoleum in brown / green / yellow geometric patterns; bare concrete in shops.
- Ceilings: acoustic tile, low; exposed joists, pipes and ducts in basements;
  older main-street stores pressed-tin (2' x 2' / 2' x 4' plates, 1890s-1930s)
  [nps.gov preservation brief 49]; work buildings open framing / trusses.
- Light: bare bulbs with pull chains, fluorescent battens in shops, table lamps,
  mushroom lamps (smoked / amber glass).
- Kitchens: avocado green / harvest gold appliances, dark wood cabinets,
  arborite counters, chrome dinette sets, percolator, wall clock.
- Living: brown / plaid / floral sofas, console TV in a wood cabinet (small
  screen), portable B/W TV with rabbit ears, stereo with wood speakers, LPs in
  milk crates, board games, beanbags, macrame plant hangers.
- Basements: furnace and ducts, oil tank, laundry (olive washer / dryer, utility
  sink, ironing board behind a curtain), second fridge, card table and folding
  chairs, bar with vinyl stools, ping-pong / pool table, support poles.
  [cheapism.com 1970s basement; apartmenttherapy.com 1970s house features]
- Phones: rotary wall phones and desk phones (beige, harvest gold, black),
  long cords; payphones outside.
- Magazines and paper: stacks of magazines, newspapers, flight log books,
  aeronautical charts, clipboards, carbon-copy waybills.

Bush flying base contents (typical of the time, generic):
- Hangar: corrugated steel or board-and-batten, big sliding doors, open wood
  trusses, workbench with vise, pegboard of tools, parts shelves, oil cases,
  avgas drums and a hand pump, engine hoist, compressor, tool chest, creeper,
  tail stand, spare prop, skis / floats / big tyres stored, platform scale for
  cargo, hand truck, cargo (boxes, sacks, drums), tie-downs, chocks.
- Living corner: a plywood room or just a corner - cot with a wool blanket,
  footlocker, oil space heater or barrel stove, table and chair, hot plate,
  percolator, kettle, water jug and basin, small fridge, shelf of cans, radio
  (VHF base, HF set, CB), rotary phone, portable TV, magazines, calendar, wall
  chart with pins, clock, coat hooks with parka / flight jacket, rifle rack.
- Volunteer forest firefighter: hand tools (Pulaski - axe and adze head,
  McLeod rake / hoe, round-point shovels), 5-gal backpack pumps, portable fire
  pump with suction hose, rolled 1.5" forestry hose, hard hats, yellow shirts,
  first aid kit. Roadside fire danger rating sign: LOW / MODERATE / HIGH /
  EXTREME on a dial with an arrow, set daily [firesmartbc.ca].
- Outside: windsock, white painted tyre runway markers, fuel drums on a rack,
  outhouse, burn barrel, propane tank, power and phone line on wood poles,
  mailbox and a hand-painted sign at the road, a pickup, picnic table.

HOPE VALLEY TERRAIN - references (DESIGN_NOTES 226)
===================================================
Dave: a mountain town that feels real and full of features - no big flat
plains; mountainous curving roads, lakes and rivers; a river flowing out of a
modelled cave entrance in a mountain; later a crack in a mountain you can dig
into, a deep cavern and an underground river through a modelled cave. His
strip should be difficult, in the mountains. Scale must feel right next to
the 1.8 m character and the vehicles - compact and detailed, not huge.

Real Hope, BC (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope,_British_Columbia):
- At the confluence of the Fraser and the Coquihalla, elevation ~41 m.
- Southern end of the Fraser Canyon (Coast Mountains north-west); Cascades
  east and south. Significant peaks close north, east and south of the
  townsite. Flat land ONLY to the west (the Fraser floodplain starts here).
- Coquihalla River: rises at the Coquihalla Lakes, drops ~1,000 m in 53 km
  (~19 m/km) through a deep narrow valley, "tumultuous ... incessant roar";
  mouth at Hope 38 m. Coquihalla Canyon east of town: narrow gorge, the
  Othello Tunnels (old railway tunnels through the canyon walls).
- Kawkawa Lake: a lake community just east of town.
- Highways 1, 3, 5, 7 meet at Hope; CP and CN railways both pass through.
- Oceanic climate, ~2,000 mm precipitation, cloudy much of the year.
- Hope Slide (1965), en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope_Slide: 47 million m3 off
  Johnson Peak's south-west face, 2 km wide, fell ~1,200 m, debris up to
  150 m deep over the highway; the scar is still mostly bare rock.
- Hope Aerodrome: turf, 1,207 m, 4.8 km west of town in Flood.

Forest (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_mainland_coastal_forests):
- Low: western hemlock, western red cedar, amabilis fir, Douglas fir (south
  coast). Bigleaf maple, vine maple, red alder in gullies, slides, riverbanks
  (yellow-orange in fall).
- High: mountain hemlock, amabilis fir, yellow cedar; above treeline alpine
  meadows, sedge, lichen-covered rock. Logged valleys: 1970s-80s clearcuts
  and logging roads switchbacking up the slopes.

Mountain features to model (from the photos and the above):
- Steep forested walls rising right behind town; rock bluffs and cliff bands
  (grey granite), talus / scree fans below them, avalanche chutes (treeless
  strips of alder and slide brush down the slopes), creek gullies with
  waterfalls, a bare slide scar, snow above ~1,500 m, rocky summits.
- Benches and knolls at the foot of the slopes; the river's gravel bars;
  a narrow side canyon with a railway tunnel.
- Lakes: a lake in a basin just east of town; small tarns higher up.
- A creek coming out of a cave mouth at the foot of a limestone cliff (a
  karst resurgence): an overhanging dark opening, the creek pouring out over
  boulders, moss, spray.

Mountain strip (flyingmag.com/nowhere-to-hide, mountainflying.com backcountry
landings):
- Short, sloped: land uphill, take off downhill; often one-way with no
  go-around; timber crowding the sides and ends; surfaces dirt / gravel /
  grass, soft and hard spots; often hidden until short final behind terrain;
  canyon and creek winds that shift near the ground.

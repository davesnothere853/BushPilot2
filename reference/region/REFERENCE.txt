REGION, WEATHER AND YARDS - references (DESIGN_NOTES 227)
=========================================================
Dave: a big area with a few SMALL towns in different areas; fly through the
mountains every which way, not one valley over and over; proper yards with
fences; dynamic weather and fog in different places - fog settling in a
valley. Same mountain heights as now, the map a lot bigger, roads a logging
truck could drive. Draft: region2.png (a web of valleys and passes, 4 towns).

Small towns (models):
- Talkeetna, AK (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talkeetna_Historic_District): founded
  1916 as an Alaska Railroad construction HQ, then home for miners; a few
  blocks round Main Street; one- and two-storey wood-frame and log buildings
  (the 1917 log roadhouse, an inn, the first schoolhouse); river junction.
- McCarthy / Kennecott, AK: a tiny bush town at the end of a long gravel road,
  a footbridge over a glacial river, a gravel strip beside town, the old red
  mill buildings stacked up the mountainside, a glacier at the valley head.
- Hope, BC (reference/hope_terrain): the river-junction town, highways meeting.
- Cannery / fishing villages: docks on pilings, cannery sheds over the water,
  float planes at the dock, boardwalks, a beach or tide-flat landing.

Mountain passes (airfactsjournal.com Merrill Pass; adn.com Lake Clark Pass):
- Narrow notches between peaks too steep to hold plants; tight turns; side
  canyons that look like the way through but dead-end; a rock saddle to climb
  over; often too narrow to turn round once committed; old wrecks in them;
  rain and dark cut the visibility to the ground under each wing.

Mountain weather (CAP mountain flying weather module; nps.gov/romo clouds and
fog; weather.gov valley fog):
- Valley fog: clear calm nights - cold air drains off the slopes and pools in
  the valley bottoms, the river adds moisture; it thickens through the night,
  deepest at daybreak, from above a white branching pattern filling the
  valleys ("white veins") with the ridges sticking out; it burns off within a
  few hours after sunrise, usually gone by mid-morning; it doesn't drift.
- Evening fog/low cloud = things getting worse; morning fog = improving.
  Ceilings in valleys can drop very fast; terrain sticks up into them.
- Up-valley breeze by day, down-valley breeze in the evening and night.
- Cap clouds sitting on the summits (windward side, dissolving on the lee),
  lenticular (lens) clouds over ridges in strong wind with rotor turbulence
  under them; lee-side downdrafts; afternoon cumulus building over the peaks.
- Coast: sea fog rolling in over the cold water and up the river mouths.

Yards (1970s-80s, small towns and homesteads):
- Fences: white picket at the front of town houses, wire mesh on wood posts,
  chain-link, split-rail / log rail fences on homesteads, gates.
- Lawns, vegetable gardens, a clothesline, a swing set, a doghouse or a dog
  yard, a tool shed, a woodpile under a lean-to, a raised log cache (Denali's
  Pearson cabin: ~3 m up on posts, a removable ladder), a fuel tank on a
  stand, a boat on a trailer, an old truck or car on blocks, a gravel drive,
  a mailbox at the road.

Harbor fishing town by the sea, mountains behind (Dave: "go to the ocean
like that with some mountains, a harbor fishing town by the sea").
Model: Cordova, AK (cityofcordova.net; sah-archipedia.org Cordova):
- On an inlet of the sea with a mountain rising straight above town; a lake
  just behind it (float planes use the lake and the boat harbor).
- Platted as a grid: four main streets parallel to the water, stacked one
  above another up a steep hillside - steep cross streets climbing from the
  waterfront.
- A small boat harbor behind a rock breakwater (built 1938), the fishing
  fleet, fish plants and canneries on pilings along the shore, a dock where
  the old railroad ended, a shipyard with a travel lift.
- A short gravel municipal strip (about 550 m) near town; the long airport far
  out; one gravel road out along the delta that doesn't go anywhere now.
- Temperate rainforest: very wet (about 4,200 mm a year), low cloud, snow.
Also Seward, AK: harbor at the head of a fjord, mountains straight out of the
water, a glacier at the valley head.
In the region: the river-mouth town becomes this harbor town at the head of a
fjord - the sea, the mountains straight up out of it, sea fog in the mornings.

DAVE'S TWO PHOTOS - "just like that" (dave_alps_haze.png, dave_canyon_fog.png)
- Aerial: ridge after ridge of steep forested mountains in every direction,
  sharp-ish summits, 1,000-1,500 m above narrow flat valley floors; the
  ridges fade blue-grey into haze with distance (aerial perspective), layers
  of silhouettes. A valley floor ~0.5-1 km wide: a patchwork of green fields
  and hedgerows, a village strung along a winding road and a river, patches of
  forest; high meadows and clearings on the shoulders; a lake glinting far
  off; low mist lying in the far valleys.
- Canyon: a deep gorge with sheer grey rock walls hundreds of metres high,
  conifers on the ledges and rims, the floor filled with a river of fog,
  peaks fading into the light beyond.
